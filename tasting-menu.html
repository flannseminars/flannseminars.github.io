The proposed pilot process
01 / BRING A PAPER

Read it. Recommend it.

Nominate a paper from the previous cycle. Tell us what caught your attention and who should read it. Self-nominations are welcome and clearly marked.

02 / MAKE THE CASE

Put your name behind it.

Recommend a paper for +1. Add a specific, detailed endorsement for +2: why it matters, what you checked, and a useful caveat.

03 / SERVE A SMALL MENU

Make time to read.

After the window closes, editors choose up to five qualifying papers. Each gets a short guide, a video when available, and attributed community recommendations.

What makes a strong recommendation?

Specificity. Explain an idea, result, or use that convinced you (at least 120 characters), what you read or checked (60), and a limitation or boundary (40). These are completeness checks; editors still read the substance. Extra adjectives do not earn extra weight. An ordinary +1 only needs a reason.

How do votes and self-nominations work?

One recommendation per person per paper; your latest valid recommendation replaces the earlier one. A strong independent recommendation counts as two points in total. Authors, coauthors, and other declared conflicts can contribute at most one point and cannot satisfy the independent-support requirement. GitHub reactions are not counted. Names and relationships are public; organizers review suspicious activity and undisclosed conflicts.

Which papers make the menu?

A paper needs at least two independent readers, including one strong endorsement, to qualify for consideration. Editors consider the reasons, relevance, and variety across the whole window, then publish their choices and short selection notes. Scores inform the decision; they do not automatically decide it. We may publish fewer than five. Unselected nominations remain visible, and absence from a menu is not a judgment of quality.

When do we do this?

Proposed rhythm: January, May, and September, each looking back at the preceding four months. Two weeks for nominations and recommendations, followed by a week for editing. Exact pilot dates and the eligible paper window will be announced before opening.

What will be public?

Your GitHub name, paper, relationship, and recommendation are public. By contributing through this form, you agree that FLaNN may quote your recommendation with attribution and a link to the original. Corrections and substantive caveats are welcome in the public thread. Moderators may remove spam, abuse, and private information; selection decisions remain explainable.
