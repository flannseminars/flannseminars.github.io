The proposed pilot process
+1 “I recommend this.”+5 “I’m really excited about this.”
20 points to qualify

20 recommendations · 4 enthusiastic endorsements · or a mix

01 / RECOMMEND IN PUBLIC

Put your enthusiasm into words.

Write freely about why you believe a paper is good. What could it change or make possible? Even a +1 needs at least a line. Self-nominations are welcome and clearly marked.

02 / LET THE COMMUNITY DECIDE

Reach the public threshold.

A score of at least 20 qualifies. Everyone can see the names, comments, and votes on GitHub. The whole announced window counts.

03 / CHECK, INVITE & SHARE

Bring the comments together.

Hosts check the final results for duplicates, conflicts, and foul play, then invite authors to contribute a guide or video if interested. The readers’ comments can carry the highlight on their own.

What should I write?

Why do you believe this is a good paper? Write in your own voice. Its implications, the doors it could open, or why it matters to you are all welcome. There is no required summary, technical explanation, checklist, or caveat. Both levels need at least a line (20 characters); an enthusiastic +5 is an invitation to say more.

How do recommendations and self-nominations count?

One counted position per person per paper; your latest valid contribution replaces an earlier one. A recommendation is +1, an enthusiastic endorsement +5 in total. Authors, coauthors, and other declared conflicts can contribute at most +1. GitHub reactions do not count. Names, relationships, and comments are public.

What happens at the cutoff?

Every paper with at least 20 points after the hosts’ checks qualifies. There is no additional editorial ranking. We aim for a small menu, roughly five papers, and can change the threshold for a future announced edition if necessary. The current edition’s rules stay fixed.

What do the hosts do after voting?

Freeze the public results, check duplicate identities and nominations, review declared relationships and reports of foul play, and explain any corrections. Then contact the authors of qualifying papers to offer an optional written or video guide. An author’s participation is not a condition for being highlighted.

When do we do this?

Proposed rhythm: January, May, and September, each looking back at the preceding four months. Two weeks for public nominations and recommendations, then host checks and author invitations. Exact pilot dates and the eligible paper window will be announced before opening.

How are comments attributed?

A highlight shows the recommender’s name or GitHub handle, their relationship to the paper, their recommendation level, and a link to the original public comment. By contributing through this form, you agree that FLaNN may quote your comment with attribution. Hosts preserve its meaning and relevant context. Earlier editions keep their own comments and final results in the archive.
