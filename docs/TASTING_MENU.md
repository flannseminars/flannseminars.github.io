# FLaNN Tasting Menu

Three times a year, the FLaNN community helps each other find papers worth spending time with. Readers nominate papers, put their names behind their recommendations, and say why they believe the work is good. Papers reaching the published score threshold qualify for a highlight after hosts validate the public results.

The pilot aims for roughly five highlights. Five is a planning target, not a cap: every paper that meets the published rules qualifies. A highlight can stand on the recommenders' own words, with a paper link and optional written or video guidance from its authors. The full nomination list and previous editions remain available in the archive.

## The pilot's rules

| Contribution | Points | What to provide |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Recommendation | +1 | A public, signed comment explaining why you believe this is a good paper. |
| Enthusiastic endorsement | +5 | The same freeform comment, with the stronger recommendation selected because you feel strongly about the paper. |
| Author, coauthor, or other declared conflict | At most +1 | The same comment, with your relationship disclosed. |
| Reaction or invalid submission | 0 | GitHub reactions are welcome but do not count as recommendations. |

**A validated score of 20 or more qualifies a paper for this edition.** Four +5 endorsements, twenty +1 recommendations, or a mixture can reach the threshold. A +5 endorsement is five points in total. There is no additional independent-reader quota and no discretionary ranking among qualifying papers.

Both levels ask one question: **“Why do you believe this is a good paper?”** Write at least a line in your own words. You might discuss why the work matters or what its implications could be; the format is up to you. There is no required summary, result, list of checks, or caveat. The 20-character minimum is only a completeness check, not a judgment about the quality of a recommendation.

Recommend papers you have genuinely spent time with. Choose +5 when it reflects your own conviction. You can recommend multiple papers, including your own, and there is no per-person budget of enthusiastic endorsements in the pilot.

One person has one counted position per paper. The latest valid contribution from a GitHub account replaces that account's earlier contribution, including the recommendation in the original nomination. Multiple comments, duplicate nominations, and arXiv versions do not multiply that person's vote. Hosts check suspected duplicate identities before publishing final results.

Declare authorship and relevant relationships, such as involvement in the work, advising, close collaboration, or a direct commercial stake. Choose `Author / coauthor` or `Other conflict of interest` where appropriate; even a requested +5 then counts as +1. Same-institution affiliation alone is not automatically a conflict; disclose it when it could reasonably affect interpretation. Hosts document uncertain cases in the public validation record.

The score measures recommendations under these rules. It is not a scientific quality score or a claim of verified expert consensus. A paper below the threshold has not received a negative scientific judgment.

## Nomination window and annual rhythm

Publish the paper window, nomination dates, time zone, scoring rules, and hosts before opening the call. A practical pilot is two weeks for nominations and recommendations, followed by a week for validation and publication. Optional author contributions can arrive later without delaying the highlights.

| Edition | Suggested paper window | Nominations | Validation and publication |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| January | September–December of the previous year | Two announced weeks in January | Following week |
| May | January–April | Two announced weeks in May | Following week |
| September | May–August | Two announced weeks in September | Following week |

These months are a proposed rhythm, not scheduled deadlines. A clearly announced conference cycle can replace the calendar paper window. Choose the pilot's exact dates before opening it.

For the pilot, publicly accessible papers, including preprints, are eligible when their first public version or a substantive new version falls in the announced window. Provide a stable link and version date, and explain any substantive version update in the nomination. Hosts resolve borderline cases against the published eligibility rule and document the outcome.

Evaluate the complete nomination window. Reaching 20 early is a provisional result, not a reason to close the call or reserve one of five slots. If many or few papers qualify, publish that result and consider a different threshold for a future edition. Never change the current round's threshold to force a particular number of highlights.

## Posting a recommendation

Each paper has one canonical public GitHub issue. Search existing nominations before opening another. Anyone with a GitHub account can nominate a paper; the issue form supplies its details and initial recommendation. Use the website's recommendation flow or this comment template to support an existing nomination.

Your GitHub name, relationship, and recommendation are public. By posting through the supplied forms or this template, you agree that FLaNN may quote your comment with attribution and a link to the original. Do not include private information.

```markdown
<!-- flann-tasting-menu -->
### Relationship
Independent reader

### Recommendation
Strongly recommend (+5)

### Why do you believe this is a good paper?
[Write at least a line in your own words about why you believe this paper is good.]
```

Use one relationship value: `Independent reader`, `Author / coauthor`, or `Other conflict of interest`. Use one recommendation value: `Recommend (+1)` or `Strongly recommend (+5)`. Replace the bracketed instruction with your comment. Both levels require at least 20 characters after whitespace normalization; placeholders and missing comments do not count.

Nominations and comments share the same one-person, one-paper rule. A nomination is not an extra vote on top of its author's recommendation. Ordinary discussion remains welcome, but only a valid structured contribution changes the tally.

## What hosts do after the call closes

Hosts validate the agreed rules and publish the community's results. They do not choose a preferred top five, rerank papers by personal taste, or exclude a qualifying paper to balance topics. Use two hosts for disputed validation decisions where possible, and recuse a host with a material conflict from that decision.

1. **Close and capture.** Close the window and save its final public snapshot. Mark the tally provisional during validation.
2. **Deduplicate.** Link duplicate nominations to a canonical issue and preserve their discussion. The importer combines arXiv versions and arXiv-issued DOI aliases. A separate publisher DOI and arXiv link may need their Paper URL fields normalized to one canonical link; cross-links alone do not merge votes.
3. **Check validity.** Check paper-window eligibility, timing, duplicate identities, declared relationships, and evidence of foul play. Apply the published rules consistently; enthusiasm itself does not need to pass an editorial taste test.
4. **Explain changes.** Publish vote corrections and exclusions with concise reasons and discussion links, while protecting private information. Show the validated score and which papers meet the threshold.
5. **Publish the results.** Every valid paper at or above 20 qualifies. Preserve all valid nominations, including those below the threshold. Freeze the validated tally and record the publication date and hosts.
6. **Invite optional author material.** After the results are public, organizers may contact qualifying authors to invite a written guide, video, or existing talk. A contribution is optional and does not determine qualification or publication.
7. **Publish the highlights.** A paper link and genuine attributed recommendations are enough for a useful post. Add author guidance when available and record later corrections transparently.

GitHub accounts and self-declared relationships provide attribution, not verified real-world identity. Check credible concerns about duplicate accounts, impersonation, or undisclosed conflicts without presenting the tally as an identity audit. Explain adjustments publicly instead of silently changing results, and keep private evidence out of the public record.

## What a highlight contains

- **Paper details:** title, authors, public link, and version/date.
- **Community recommendations:** accurate comments or excerpts with each recommender's name, declared relationship, and original public contribution link.
- **Result:** the edition's scoring rules, validated score, and public validation record.
- **Optional author guide:** a written introduction, video, or linked talk, clearly attributed to its contributor.

Hosts do not need to write a substitute mini-review or require a summary, key result, or limitation section. The recommenders' enthusiasm can carry the page. Omit empty optional sections. If an invitation is actually pending, a factual note can say so; do not imply that authors supplied material when they have not.

The website form requires acknowledgment of public posting and attributed quotation; the issue form and comment template disclose the same terms. Contributors using these flows have already agreed, so hosts need not request the same permission again. Verify permission when importing contributions supplied outside those flows. Preserve meaning and attribution when excerpting. Prefer linking to existing videos; obtain permission before republishing or editing a recording.

The linked GitHub issue is the discussion area. Corrections, questions, and substantive disagreement can stay alongside the original recommendations.

## Moderation, corrections, and examples

Keep discussion about the paper. Remove spam, abuse, and private information under ordinary repository moderation. Explain exclusions and vote adjustments where practical without amplifying sensitive material. Use the canonical issue for corrections and link any resulting website pull request.

Later recommendations do not silently change a published edition. A factual correction should show what changed and why. If a contributor withdraws permission for a website excerpt, remove it and keep the minimum correction record needed; the original GitHub content remains subject to GitHub's controls and the contributor's choices.

Preview examples must be visibly illustrative. Mock recommendations use names such as **Example reader A**, never a real person's name attached to invented praise. Mock scores are not community results. An illustrative author-guide placeholder must say it is a mock and must not imply that the authors supplied material. Preview examples are excluded from live publication.

## Pipeline and activation

The static-site pipeline is: public GitHub issues and comments → read-only sync → a reviewable JSON snapshot → host validation and publication data → generated highlights and edition archives. Nothing automatically contacts authors, sends an announcement, casts votes, or publishes an edition.

The checked-in pilot is in **planning / preview mode**. Opening a local page or merging the implementation does not itself open a nomination window. Activate it after the dates and rules are ready.

`data/tasting-menu.json` contains the repository and active edition configuration. The pilot uses:

```json
{
  "targetPapers": 5,
  "scoring": {
    "recommendation": 1,
    "endorsement": 5,
    "threshold": 20,
    "minCommentLength": 20
  }
}
```

`targetPapers` is a planning goal, not a publication limit. The nomination snapshot includes `scoring` so the rules behind its result are auditable. Once an edition has a timestamped snapshot, its scoring is fixed; announce changes under a new edition ID. Keep the same rules when refreshing or correcting an existing edition.

`data/tasting-menu-editorial.json` contains two arrays: `editions` and `papers`. Each edition records its ID, title, publication date, frozen scoring, public results URL, and validation note. Each paper references an edition and carries its bibliography, support counts and score, attributed recommendations, and a host note about validation. Written introductions and author guides are optional. A real published paper needs a qualifying score and at least one genuine sourced recommendation to quote. This is presentation of the qualifying result, not a new voting requirement.

Keep generated nominations separate from publication material so a sync cannot overwrite a highlight. Mark illustrative editions, papers, quotes, and guides with `example: true`. The builder excludes preview records when `preview` is false.

The generator builds `tasting-menu.html`, `tasting-menu/archive.html`, individual `tasting-menu/editions/<edition>.html` archive pages, and `tasting-menu/papers/<id>.html` highlights. Build from the repository root:

```sh
python3 scripts/build_tasting_menu.py
```

Sync and check the pipeline with:

```sh
python3 scripts/tasting_menu.py sync
python3 scripts/tasting_menu.py check
python3 scripts/tasting_menu.py test
python3 -m unittest discover -s tests -v
python3 scripts/build_tasting_menu.py --check
```

The sync needs network access to GitHub. Consult its help for supported options and authentication. It reads issues and comments, writes a local snapshot, and does not mutate GitHub. Review snapshot changes and generated pages through a pull request before merging them into the website.

Before activation, enable Issues, create the `tasting-menu` label, and merge the issue template and workflow onto the default branch. Manually dispatched workflows must be on that branch. Configure the edition, paper window, and explicit opening and closing timestamps; set `preview` to `false` and the status to `open` when the call is ready.

The native GitHub issue form applies the nomination label. Website-created drafts use ordinary issue creation, because participants may lack label permissions. Hosts must label valid incoming `[Tasting Menu]` issues `tasting-menu` before the sync includes them. This is public intake housekeeping, not a discretionary selection stage.

The **Tasting Menu checks and preview snapshot** workflow can run with `sync: true` to produce a reviewable artifact. It does not commit or publish the snapshot. Closing an issue does not exclude a nomination. For withdrawn, rejected, or spam submissions, use `tasting-menu-withdrawn`, `tasting-menu-rejected`, or `tasting-menu-spam` and explain the action where appropriate. Valid papers below 20 remain visible without exclusion labels.

The site displays a snapshot, not a live tally. Refresh it during the call, after closing, and after validation. Creation time determines whether contributions were within the window and which valid contribution is latest. GitHub returns current edited text; it cannot reconstruct exactly what a comment said at the deadline. Review late edits visibly and freeze the final record rather than treating a later sync as a historical reconstruction.

The sync refuses to run when an edition is `published`. For an intentional correction, temporarily set it to `review`, inspect the diff, and restore `published` before merging. Preserve the edition's scoring and explain corrected contributions.

Before changing the active edition, copy its final reviewed snapshot to `data/tasting-menu-archive/<edition>.json`. Keep previous edition and paper entries with their original IDs in the publication file. Their static pages retain highlights, rules, validation notes, and links to public results. Never overwrite the only copy of an earlier edition's contributions or results.

## First-cycle checklist

- [ ] Announce the paper window, dates and time zone, hosts, +1/+5 weights, and threshold of 20.
- [ ] Agree on conflict handling and who resolves disputed validation decisions.
- [ ] Merge templates and workflow; enable Issues and create the nomination label.
- [ ] Configure the edition and exclude preview examples from live publication.
- [ ] Test both recommendation levels, duplicate nominations, and conflict downgrades. Apply `tasting-menu-withdrawn` to test issues and refresh the snapshot; closing alone does not exclude them.
- [ ] Check mobile and desktop views, submission links, and the archive.
- [ ] Open the call and publish the prepared announcement through the organizers' usual channels.
- [ ] Label valid incoming nominations, consolidate duplicates, answer public questions, and refresh the snapshot.
- [ ] At the deadline, close the round, capture the results, and perform validation checks.
- [ ] Publish validation outcomes and all qualifying papers; retain all valid nominations.
- [ ] Optionally invite qualifying authors to contribute written or video guidance after results are public.
- [ ] Publish highlights using genuine recommendations; do not wait for optional author material.
- [ ] Preserve the final snapshot, publication record, and archive before the next edition.
- [ ] Review participation, qualifying-paper count, reader feedback, and host workload; change the threshold only for a future announced edition.

`docs/tasting-menu-call.txt` is a draft announcement. Replace its bracketed placeholders before sending. No announcement or author invitation has been sent by this implementation.

When changing the threshold for a future edition, give it a new edition ID, regenerate the site, and update the call and GitHub issue-form wording to match. Earlier edition records retain their original scoring rules.
